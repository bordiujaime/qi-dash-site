Methodology

From data to a clear,
repeatable selection.

Our proprietary algorithm ranks opportunities using defined rules. A monthly review updates the five positions to keep the process consistent over time.

01 / HISTORY

Study the full record

The methodology is studied using historical series beginning in 2006/2007. Retrospective analysis helps show how it would have behaved in different periods, without assuming the future will repeat the past.

02 / SIGNAL

Apply the algorithm

A proprietary algorithm processes the data and produces a quantitative ranking. The signal becomes a monthly selection of five stocks.

03 / TRACKING

Review and rebalance

The portfolio is reassessed each month. Positions are updated according to the new selection, creating a sequence of decisions that can be tracked and analysed.

Academic context

Return persistence is an active research topic.

Peer-reviewed research has documented momentum in stocks and across asset classes. This provides context for quantitative approaches based on trends or relative strength, but does not prove or validate Q Investment’s specific algorithm.

Jegadeesh & Titman (1993) studied stock strategies that buy recent winners and sell recent losers.

Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012) examined time-series momentum in equity-index, currency, commodity and bond futures.

How to read this comparison: it is an internal calculation using the historical windows available in the dashboard, not an independent audit or a return promise. Multi-year windows overlap; results depend on the model’s data and assumptions and may not include all costs, taxes or execution slippage. The two-year real-world tracking period is reported separately from the historical analysis. Past performance does not guarantee future results.

Sources: Aswath Damodaran, Annual Returns on Stocks, 1928–2018 (dividends included; page last updated January 2019); The Journal of Finance; Journal of Financial Economics.
