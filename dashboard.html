Separate proposal in the report: 51% ISI + 49% S&P 500

The document describes an estimated ~92% probability of beating the S&P 500 and about 0.4× average excess terminal value. It also says the mix improves left-tail capital preservation versus pure ISI, with drawdown roughly 2–3 percentage points above the pure index.

Use with caution: the PDF does not give a horizon-by-horizon numeric table or percentile ranges for this mix, so these claims are kept separate from the quantitative comparison above.

How the simulation works and what its uncertainty means
  • Historical bootstrap resamples observed monthly returns; it preserves their observed distribution but breaks their sequence, which can understate clustered drawdowns.
  • The parametric Student-t model allows heavier tails; the report's 5th-percentile drawdown is generally more severe under this model.
  • For strategy-versus-index comparisons, the report uses shared resampling indices so both experience the same simulated market scenarios.
  • P5–P95 is the central 90% of simulated final outcomes under the chosen historical window and model assumptions. It is not a confidence interval, nor a guarantee about future returns.

These simulations are from the April 2026 report and are not recalculated from the live monthly dashboard data.

How to read the dates and values: “First selected” is the dashboard recommendation month. The starting figure is the earliest adjusted close stored for that ticker in the workbook; the ending figure is its latest stored adjusted close. Total cumulative growth

Equal-share basket return, as a percentage of its combined starting value.

Average growth per stock

Arithmetic average of each included ticker’s percentage change.

How these figures are calculated: Each point for a start year includes distinct tickers whose first recommendation falls from that start year through the selected final year. Total growth is the sum of dollar changes (latest snapshot value minus first stored reference value) divided by the sum of starting values, assuming one share per ticker. Average growth is the arithmetic mean of the individual percentage changes. These are different summaries: the total uses the combined starting value as its denominator, while the average gives each ticker equal weight. Tickers without a recorded starting price are excluded. Values use the point-in-time price snapshot above, exclude dividends and trading costs, and are not a simulated portfolio return.
