QI FUNDS · METHODOLOGY AND RESULTS

The Qi Strategy

Qi starts with a systematic stock-selection process and combines it in three different ways. This creates portfolios with different levels of equity exposure. We begin with the process, then explain what the results show and what to keep in mind when reading them.

General information, not investment advice. This page describes a theoretical proposal based on historical data. It is not personalised advice, an offer, solicitation or invitation to buy, sell or hold financial instruments. Each person should assess their own circumstances, objectives and risk tolerance and, where appropriate, consult an authorised professional.

The starting point is the Inertial Synthetic Index (ISI), a quantitative system that ranks U.S. large-cap stocks each month by the relative persistence of their recent behaviour. The resulting selection is combined with other assets to build portfolios that are more defensive, balanced or equity-oriented.

Monthly review Quantitative selection Universe: S&P 500 · Russell 1000 · Nasdaq 100
How a signal becomes a portfolio ⓘ

The ISI compares stocks with one another: it assigns scores and produces a ranking, but does not aim to predict how much each company will rise or fall. At each monthly review, the strategy selects the n highest-ranked stocks and systematically updates the portfolio.

The presentation names the S&P 500, Russell 1000 and Nasdaq 100 as reference universes. It does not set out every filter, weighting or execution rule here, so the full documentation is needed to reproduce the process.

It also says a possible survivorship-bias effect is unconfirmed and needs further investigation. The presentation considers the trades executable at reasonable position sizes; that conclusion depends on the data and execution assumptions used and is not an independent audit.

FROM SIGNAL TO PORTFOLIO

Three steps each month

The logic can be summarised as compare, select and decide how the portfolio is allocated across its components.

01

Rank

The ISI scores stocks in its reference universe by the relative persistence of their recent behaviour.

02

Select

The strategy takes the highest-ranked stocks and reviews its positions in the next monthly cycle.

03

Combine

The portion not allocated to Qi is split between cash and the S&P 500 according to the chosen structure: Takeoff, Cruise or Zenith.

THREE PORTFOLIO STRUCTURES

One signal, different exposures

The ranges change the asset mix; the results below compare a 40% Qi allocation.

01 · TAKEOFF

Qi + cash

After setting the Qi allocation, the remainder stays in a cash asset. This structure has less equity exposure than the other two, though it does not remove the risk of loss.

Illustrative profile: defensive to dynamic. Takeoff 40: beta 0.63 and maximum drawdown −24.0% in the 248-month comparison.
02 · CRUISE

Qi + S&P 500 + cash

The capital not allocated to Qi is split equally between the S&P 500 and a cash asset. It is a middle-ground mix: it retains equity exposure while also holding a cash allocation.

Illustrative profile: moderate to growth. Cruise 40: beta 0.93 and maximum drawdown −37.0%.
03 · ZENITH

Qi + S&P 500

The rest of the portfolio is invested in the S&P 500. Of the three structures, it keeps the greatest exposure to equities.

Illustrative profile: aggressive to very aggressive. Zenith 40: beta 1.23 and maximum drawdown −49.0%.

READING THE METRICS

What each measure means

ISI

Inertial Synthetic Index: a proprietary score that ranks stocks by the relative persistence of their recent behaviour. It is a ranking, not a return forecast.

Beta

The historical relationship between a portfolio’s movements and the S&P 500. A beta of 1 suggests similar sensitivity during the measured period, but does not summarise every risk.

Sharpe

Compares excess return with return variability using a reference rate and stated calculation assumptions. It summarises a historical relationship; it does not measure every risk.

Calmar

Relates annualised return to maximum drawdown, helping put return in context with the largest recorded decline.

Maximum drawdown

The largest fall from an earlier peak to a later low. A 24% decline takes 100 to 76; it then takes a rise of more than 24% to return to 100.

Volatility

How much returns vary. A higher value means wider historical swings, up and down; it does not indicate future direction.

UW · Underwater

Time spent below a previous peak. The table reports the average length of these recovery periods in months.

Backtest

A simulation of how a strategy would have performed using historical data and rules. Results depend on data quality and execution assumptions.

“Auditable”

The presentation’s label for March 2024–August 2026 (30 months). The term alone does not show that an independent review took place.

How to read the results as a whole. The tables describe historical performance and do not guarantee future results. In the main comparison, the presentation sets Cash = 0%, so no cash return is added.
